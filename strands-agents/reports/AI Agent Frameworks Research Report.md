AI agent frameworks are platforms or software environments that provide tools, libraries, and infrastructure to develop AI agents. AI agents are systems that can perceive their environment, make decisions, and act autonomously or semi-autonomously.[source]

Types of AI Agent Frameworks:

1. Reactive Frameworks: These frameworks are designed to react to stimuli from the environment in real-time. They are often used in applications requiring quick decision-making, such as robotics and video games.[source]

2. Deliberative Frameworks: These frameworks involve a more thoughtful process, where the agent considers multiple options and their potential outcomes before making a decision. They are suitable for applications that require complex reasoning and planning, such as autonomous vehicles and scheduling systems.[source]

3. Hybrid Frameworks: Combining the features of both reactive and deliberative frameworks, hybrid frameworks can switch between reactive and deliberative modes depending on the situation. They are versatile and can be applied to a wide range of scenarios.[source]

4. Distributed Frameworks: These frameworks are designed to manage and coordinate multiple agents working together to achieve a common goal. They are used in applications such as multi-agent systems and swarm intelligence.[source]

Notable Examples of AI Agent Frameworks:

1. TensorFlow: An open-source platform for machine learning and deep learning.[source]
2. PyTorch: An open-source machine learning library based on the Torch library.[source]
3. Keras: A high-level neural networks API, written in Python and capable of running on top of TensorFlow, CNTK, or Theano.[source]
4. Cognitive Toolkit: A framework for developing AI agents that can learn and adapt.[source]
5. Reinforcement Learning Toolkit: A framework for developing AI agents that learn through trial and error.[source]
